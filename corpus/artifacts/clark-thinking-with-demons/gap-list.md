- [ ] `bodin-republique-1576` — Jean Bodin, *Les Six Livres de la République* (1576); theory of sovereignty; Clark's Ch. 44 argues this must be read alongside *Démonomanie* as expressions of a single intellectual vision.

- [ ] `bodin-demonomanie-1580` — Jean Bodin, *De la démonomanie des sorciers* (1580); Clark calls this "the Malleus maleficarum of the next hundred years"; extreme witch-believing position; entry for the *République* must cross-reference this.

- [ ] `molitor-tractatus-lamiis-1489` — Ulrich Molitor, *Tractatus de lamiis* (1489); notable for using dialogue form, with Archduke Sigismund voicing sceptical positions (Ch. 13); illustrates that the belief/sceptic binary was constructed dialogically within a single text.

## New `demonological_concept` entries needed

- [ ] `contrariety-universal-principle` — Aristotle's doctrine of contrariety (from *Categoriae*) as adopted by early modern culture and demonology: things are defined by their contraries; witchcraft was cognizable as the necessary opposite of all positive moral, natural, and social categories. Clark's Chs. 3–4 (pp. 47–105). Developed from the Aristotelian logical tradition but functioning as a cultural logic in early modern Europe.

- [ ] `devil-as-gods-ape` — The patristic-scholastic doctrine that the devil is God's "ape" (simia Dei): he can only imitate, parody, and invert divine creation. This doctrine guaranteed that demonic witchcraft made sense as a systematic parody — every element of the sabbat was the mirror-opposite of divine worship. Clark's Ch. 6 (pp. 106–123). ACTOR category: contemporaries' own doctrine.

- [ ] `prerogative-instances-demonology` — Clark's adaptation of Bacon's "prerogative instances" (extreme or anomalous cases in nature) to describe the epistemological role of demonological anomalies: demonic phenomena served as test cases that pushed causal classifications to their limits and thereby refined natural philosophy. Chs. 16–17 (pp. 306–339). ANALYST category: Clark's own analytical framework.

- [ ] `mystical-politics-demonological-frame` — Clark's term for the sacralized conception of political authority invoked throughout demonological writing: the magistrate as quasi-sacerdotal figure wielding divinely-ordained charismatic power against Satan's agents; grounded in divine-right theory and Romans 13. Chs. 39–40 (pp. 686–729). ANALYST category.

- [ ] `postremus-furor-satanae` — "The final fury of Satan": the early modern conviction (shared by Protestant and Catholic demonologists alike) that demonic and witching activity was intensifying in the current age because the End was near. This gave witch prosecution an eschatological urgency. Clark's Chs. 21–22 (pp. 383–415). ACTOR category.

- [ ] `witch-as-prodigy-portent` — The cultural assimilation of the witch into the early modern category of marvels, monsters, and prodigies: witches were portents to be read eschatologically alongside other wonders. Clark's Ch. 24 (pp. 432–445). ACTOR category.

- [ ] `discernment-of-spirits-demonological` — The theological art of *discretio spirituum* (discernment of spirits), which distinguished divine inspiration from demonic deception; Clark argues in Ch. 9 (pp. 171–186) that the logic of inversion created permanent anxiety here, since demonic could always masquerade as divine. ACTOR category.

- [ ] `believers-sceptics-spectrum` — Clark's analytical argument (Ch. 13, pp. 240–261) that the "believers vs. sceptics" binary imposed by modern rationalist historiography is false: demonological literature occupied a vast middle ground where specific claims about demonic capacity were evaluated individually using natural-philosophical criteria. ANALYST category.

- [ ] `witch-gender-binary-construction` — Clark's argument (Ch. 8, pp. 138–170) that the feminization of the witch was an effect of the same binary logic that structured all demonological thought: women and witches were made intelligible through shared hierarchical oppositions (reason/passion, order/disorder). Distinct from sociological explanations of women's vulnerability. ANALYST category.

- [ ] `witchcraft-political-language` — Clark's synthesis in Part V: witchcraft prosecution was embedded in a "mystical politics" that sacralized the magistracy; Bodin's sovereignty theory and his witch prosecution theory were expressions of the same intellectual vision; between divine right and demonic right there was a "natural enmity." ANALYST category.

- [ ] `providence-affliction-framework` — The Protestant pastoral framework (esp. Perkins, Gifford) that treated witchcraft affliction primarily as a test of faith under divine providence rather than a social crime requiring prosecution; derived from Reformed theology's doctrine of divine sovereignty. Clark's Ch. 30 (pp. 525–538). ACTOR category.

- [ ] `reformation-demonology-nexus` — The structural connection between Reformation dogmatics, casuistry, and catechesis and the production/intensification of demonological belief; both Protestant and Catholic reformations intensified the conceptual apparatus for identifying witchcraft. Clark's Ch. 33 (pp. 577–601). HYBRID category.

- [ ] `magistrate-inviolability-doctrine` — The doctrine that magistrates were inviolable from witchcraft by virtue of their divinely-sanctioned office (derived from Romans 13); grounded in the same mystical-political framework as divine-right theory. Clark's Ch. 38 (pp. 673–685). ACTOR category.
