About WitchcraftStudiesDB

Historical Witchcraft Studies Knowledge Portal — Digital Humanities Project

A relational JSON database, interactive website, and academic corpus covering European witch trials, demonological theory, folk practitioners, and the historiography of witchcraft from antiquity through the early nineteenth century. The project integrates three complementary scholarly traditions to produce a multi-perspectival record.

Historiographical Framework

The database applies three complementary scholarly traditions in productive tension. No single approach is privileged — all three illuminate different aspects of the historical record.

Stuart Clark

Intellectual History

Author of Thinking with Demons (1997). Clark's fundamental contribution: rather than asking "How could people believe in witches?" (presupposing irrationality), he asks "What made witchcraft beliefs coherent to educated men?" Prosecutions were not irrational panic but rational applications of learned doctrine.

Ronald Hutton

Social History

Author of The Witch (2017). Hutton emphasizes cunning folk as legitimate community practitioners who operated independently for centuries before criminalization. His social history traces the demographic and community dynamics of accusation — who accused whom, and why.

Carlo Ginzburg

Microhistory

Author of Night Battles (1966/1983) and Ecstasies (1989). Ginzburg's microhistorical analysis of interrogation records reveals how torture and leading questions shaped confessions. His benandanti discovery demonstrates that accused persons had self-understandings radically different from imposed demonological categories.

Eight Historiographical Principles

1. Provenance on every claim. Every substantive claim must have source_method + confidence rating. No unattributed assertions.
2. Rationality within framework. Witch prosecution was rational within its theological premises (Clark). The question is always: coherent to whom, and why?
3. Actor/Analyst distinction. Practitioners' own language versus modern analytical categories kept strictly separate throughout the database.
4. Regional variation as primary. There was no single "European witch hunt." Each region had distinct prosecution patterns, legal cultures, and folk traditions.
5. Microhistorical attention to individuals. Named persons, not just statistics. Individual biographies matter as historical evidence, not illustrations.
6. Material grounding. What people actually did, not just what they were accused of. Cunning folk's services were real social functions.
7. Intellectual history seriously. Demonological texts treated as serious theological documents, not as symptoms of irrationality.
8. Ambiguity preserved. Historiographical disagreement stated explicitly in every entry where it exists. Contested claims are labeled, not hidden.

Database Structure

All data stored as JSON files with consistent provenance fields on every entry:

TRIAL_EVENTDated prosecutions with execution counts, accused counts, regional context
ACCUSED_PERSONIndividual defendants with biography, torture records, confession status
DEMONOLOGICAL_CONCEPT100-term dictionary with Clark/Hutton/Ginzburg perspectives
TEXTPrimary and secondary sources with significance analysis
DEMONOLOGICAL_SCHOLARHistorian biographies with methodological analysis and major works
LOCATIONGeographic entries with coordinates for map plotting
HEALER_PRACTITIONERCunning folk, wise women, folk magicians — folk practitioners
TIMELINE_EVENTEvents from Roman antiquity to modern scholarship

Provenance & Data Quality

Every substantive entry carries:

  • source_method — How we know this (published trial record, archival, scholarly citation, reconstruction)
  • review_status — DRAFT (unreviewed) | REVIEWED (second reader) | VERIFIED (source checked against primary)
  • confidence — HIGH (well-established) | MEDIUM (some inference, scholarly debate) | LOW (sparse, speculative)
  • scholarly_disagreement — Explicit statement of where historians disagree on the entry's claims

Technical Standards

  • No frameworks: Vanilla HTML, CSS, and JavaScript only. No React, no Node, no build step.
  • JSON schema immutability: Once a field is added to an entity type, it appears in every entry of that type.
  • Slug-based IDs: All cross-references use lowercase-hyphenated slugs (e.g., agnes-sampson-north-berwick-1590).
  • Idempotent scripts: All Python scripts safe to re-run without duplicating data.
