About WitchcraftStudiesDB

Methodology, scope, and scholarly framework for this digital humanities knowledge portal.

Project Scope

WitchcraftStudiesDB is a relational knowledge database for the history of European and transatlantic witch trials, covering the period c.1428–1692. It integrates structured data about trial events, accused persons, demonological concepts, intellectual authorities, and primary sources into a searchable, cross-referenced portal.

The database draws on three complementary scholarly traditions and applies their methodologies consistently across all entries:

Three Scholarly Frameworks

Stuart Clark — Intellectual History
Thinking with Demons (1997)
Demonology as coherent theological and natural-philosophical tradition. Witch prosecution as intellectually serious — applying scholastic causation theory, not irrational superstition. The database treats demonological concepts with the same analytical seriousness Clark argues the demonologists themselves applied.
Ronald Hutton — Social History
The Witch (2017)
Cunning folk as legitimate practitioners whose community role was distinct from the accused witch. Demographic and regional analysis of trial victims. The 'refusal-quarrel-misfortune' sequence as the social mechanism of accusation. Gender and occupation as structural vulnerability factors.
Carlo Ginzburg — Microhistory
Night Battles (1983), Ecstasies (1991)
Interrogation analysis and torture-shaped confession. Recovery of subaltern voices and indigenous folk beliefs from beneath the demonological overlay. The benandanti case as model for distinguishing what practitioners believed from what prosecutors alleged against them.

Eight Working Principles

  1. Provenance on every claim — named source, not just confidence level
  2. Rationality within framework — accusers and accused acted within coherent belief systems
  3. Actor/Analyst distinction — contemporary terms in quotation marks; modern scholarly categories identified as such
  4. Regional variation as primary — no monolithic "witch hunt"; context is always specific
  5. Microhistorical attention to individuals — structural analysis must not erase personal specificity
  6. Material grounding — what people actually did, not only what was alleged
  7. Intellectual history taken seriously — demonological theory is theology, not superstition
  8. Ambiguity preserved — historiographical disagreement stated explicitly, with named scholars

Data Format

All database entries are stored as structured JSON files in a versioned git repository. Cross-references use slug-based IDs throughout — no UUIDs, no numeric foreign keys. Every entry carries provenance fields: source_method, confidence (HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW), review_status (DRAFT/REVIEWED/VERIFIED), and scholarly_disagreement naming the historians on each side of contested questions.

Confidence Levels

  • HIGH — Archival or published primary source; secure scholarly consensus
  • MEDIUM — Scholarly reconstruction from fragmentary evidence; inference required
  • LOW — Named in one source only; no independent corroboration; identity uncertain
